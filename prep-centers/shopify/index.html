Shopify is the channel where the question changes. With Amazon FBA and Walmart WFS, a prep center's job is inbound: make your units compliant with the marketplace's receiving spec and ship them in, after which Amazon or Walmart fulfills every order for you. Shopify has no equivalent inbound program. A Shopify store fulfills its own orders, so a Shopify prep center is really doing the opposite job. It stores your inventory and then, on each order that comes in, picks the items, packs them, and ships them straight to your customer under your own brand. That is order-by-order pick and pack, not marketplace inbound prep, and it changes what you should be comparing.

What separates Shopify-capable centers from one another is order integration, branded-packaging capability, per-order economics, and whether the center can also prep your marketplace inventory. What a Shopify prep center actually does

A Shopify fulfillment center receives and stores your inventory, then handles each customer order as it arrives. The workflow is pick, pack, ship: the center pulls the right SKUs, packs them with your packaging, generates a carrier label, and ships the parcel directly to the buyer, with tracking syncing back so the order shows fulfilled. Because the parcel goes to your customer rather than into a marketplace warehouse, the center often handles branded boxes, custom inserts, gift notes, and kitting, which is what direct-to-consumer brands care about. This is the same outbound, self-fulfilled flow Amazon sellers call FBM. It differs from FBA or WFS prep, where the center only makes inventory compliant and ships it in and the marketplace owns the last mile. A Shopify center owns the last mile on your behalf, so accuracy and presentation per order are what matter most.

How to choose a Shopify fulfillment center

Because the center is fulfilling orders rather than prepping inbound shipments, the comparison runs on different criteria. Order integration is first: the center needs a clean way to receive your Shopify orders, through a direct connection or a multichannel shipping tool. Prep Brothers in Billings, Montana, for example, runs FBM orders through Veeqo, the Amazon-owned shipping software, which lets it fulfill across marketplaces without direct Seller Central access. Next is accuracy and presentation, since every order ships under your brand: Pro Prep and Fulfillment in Marion, Illinois is veteran-owned, applies Lean Six Sigma, and claims a 99.9 percent order-accuracy rate. Then weigh minimums and pricing, which matter most to smaller stores. NOW Fulfillment in Liberty Center, Indiana states no subscriptions, startup fees, or minimum quantities, while the ISO 9001 certified Encore Business Group in Kentwood, Michigan quotes custom. Running Shopify and a marketplace from one center

Storing one inventory pool in a single warehouse that can both ship your Shopify orders and prep your FBA or WFS shipments removes a second receiving point and a duplicate set of fees. Frequently asked questions

What is a Shopify prep center, and how is it different from an Amazon FBA prep center?

A Shopify prep center fulfills your orders, while an FBA prep center preps your inventory to ship into Amazon. On Shopify there is no marketplace warehouse to send stock to, so the center stores your inventory and then picks, packs, and ships each individual customer order directly to the buyer under your brand. An FBA prep center instead labels and packages your units to meet Amazon's inbound spec and ships them in, after which Amazon fulfills the orders. So a Shopify center is doing outbound, order-by-order pick and pack, whereas an FBA center is doing inbound compliance prep.

How does a fulfillment center connect to my Shopify store and orders?

It varies by center, which is why it is worth confirming directly. Some connect to your Shopify order feed so new orders flow in automatically, and many work through a multichannel shipping platform that pulls in orders, prints labels, and pushes tracking back. Prep Brothers, for instance, uses Veeqo, the Amazon-owned shipping software, to handle FBM orders across marketplaces. The practical question to ask any Shopify-capable center is how your orders reach them and how tracking gets written back to your store so customers see accurate status.

Can the same center handle both my Shopify orders and my Amazon or Walmart prep?

Often yes, and that is a real reason to consolidate. Many of the 162 Shopify-capable centers in this directory also prep for Amazon FBA and Walmart WFS, so one warehouse can ship your Shopify orders out to customers and prep your marketplace inventory in, all from a single inventory pool. That removes a second receiving point and a duplicate set of handling fees. Prep Brothers running FBM orders through Veeqo is one example of a center built for multi-marketplace fulfillment. Filter the directory for the marketplaces you sell on to find centers that cover all of them.

Will a Shopify fulfillment center use my own branded packaging?

Many will, and for a direct-to-consumer brand it is worth asking about specifically. Because Shopify orders ship straight to your customer rather than into a marketplace warehouse, the parcel and its presentation are part of your brand experience. Centers that focus on DTC fulfillment commonly handle branded boxes, custom inserts, gift notes, and kitting or bundling, on top of the standard pick and pack. This is different from FBA or WFS prep, where the goal is plain compliance with a marketplace's labeling rules. Confirm with each center which packaging and insert options it supports and whether there is an added cost.

How is Shopify fulfillment priced compared with marketplace prep?

Shopify fulfillment is usually priced around the order rather than the prepped unit, because the center is shipping orders rather than preparing inbound shipments. In this directory the centers span several models: all-in per unit, per-service a la carte with a base fee plus itemized add-ons, and custom quotes. Storage of your inventory and the parcel carrier cost are typically separate from the pick-and-pack fee. Rather than assume a single going rate, filter by pricing model and compare each center's published terms, since outbound order fulfillment, storage, and shipping are often billed as distinct line items.

Do Shopify fulfillment centers require a minimum order volume?

Some do and some do not, which is one of the more important things for a smaller Shopify store to check. A center built for high-volume sellers may set a monthly order or unit floor, while others take any volume. NOW Fulfillment in Liberty Center, Indiana, for example, states it has no subscriptions, startup fees, or minimum quantities, which suits the small and uneven order flow a newer DTC store produces. The directory records pricing model and lets you surface no-minimum centers, so you can match a center to your actual order volume rather than overcommit.
