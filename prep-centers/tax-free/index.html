Four states have no statewide sales tax, and for the right seller that changes the math on where inventory gets prepped. Oregon, Montana, Delaware, and New Hampshire let you receive taxable goods at a prep center without paying state sales tax on the purchase, which is why online arbitrage and retail arbitrage sellers route buys through them. The saving is on the buy side, when you purchase taxable retail products and have them ship straight to a warehouse in one of these four states. It is real money on high-volume arbitrage, and it is exactly why centers like Montana Prep Solutions market the tax-free angle as offsetting their own prep fees.

Which states are tax-free, and how the saving actually works

Oregon, Montana, Delaware, and New Hampshire are the four states with no statewide sales tax that matter for prep. The saving is on the buy side: when you purchase taxable retail inventory and ship it directly to a prep center located in one of these states, there is no state sales tax added to that purchase. That is why the model fits online arbitrage and retail arbitrage sellers best, since they buy large volumes of taxable goods and the avoided tax compounds across every order. Montana Prep Solutions in Helena puts it plainly, telling clients that the tax saving alone normally pays for its prep services. Wholesale and private-label sellers usually buy under a resale certificate already, so for them the prep-state location matters more for price and logistics than for sales tax.

Oregon vs Montana vs Delaware vs New Hampshire for prep

All four states skip statewide sales tax, but they differ in supply, location logic, and other taxes. Oregon has the deepest bench of prep centers and sits on the West Coast near ports and Amazon fulfillment, so it suits import-heavy and high-volume sellers; PrepWorx, an Oregon operator, reports over one million units processed at a claimed 99.99 percent accuracy. Montana is interior, low-cost, and small-town, with centers like Prep Brothers at a confirmed Billings address and Allegra Prep n Pack in Joliet. New Hampshire borders the Massachusetts metro, making United States Prep in Salem easy to reach from Boston. One caveat: no sales tax is not no tax at all. Oregon levies a Corporate Activity Tax and Delaware has a franchise tax and gross-receipts tax, both business-level rather than per-purchase, so factor them in and confirm with a tax professional.

Frequently asked questions

Which states have no sales tax for prep purposes?

Oregon, Montana, Delaware, and New Hampshire have no statewide sales tax, so they are the four states sellers use to route taxable inventory through a prep center without paying state sales tax on the purchase. Alaska is sometimes called tax-free, but many of its boroughs levy a local sales tax, so it is generally excluded from this prep strategy. The four states above are the practical choices, and our directory groups every verified center in them on this page.

Does routing inventory through a tax-free-state prep center actually save me sales tax?

It can, on the buy side. When you purchase taxable retail products and ship them directly to a prep center in Oregon, Montana, Delaware, or New Hampshire, no state sales tax is added to that purchase. That is most valuable to online and retail arbitrage sellers buying large volumes of taxable goods. It does not change income or business taxes you owe elsewhere, and the rules depend on your own nexus and situation, so treat this as general information and confirm with a tax professional before relying on it.

Does a tax-free prep center change the sales tax my Amazon or Walmart customers pay?

No. On the sell side, Amazon and Walmart act as marketplace facilitators and collect sales tax based on where your buyer is located, not where your inventory was prepped. Moving prep to Oregon, Montana, Delaware, or New Hampshire does not reduce what your customers are charged at checkout. The tax-free benefit applies only to your own purchases of taxable inventory shipped into the prep state, never to the tax collected on your downstream marketplace sales.

Oregon or Montana for a prep center, which is better?

Both have no statewide sales tax, so the choice comes down to logistics and cost. Oregon has more prep centers and sits on the West Coast near ports and Amazon fulfillment, which suits import-heavy and higher-volume sellers, though it also levies a business-level Corporate Activity Tax. Montana is interior, lower-cost, and small-town, with operators like Prep Brothers in Billings and MT Prairie Prep N Ship that focus on smaller-item categories. Pick Oregon for scale and coast proximity, Montana for low overhead and personal service.

Do tax-free states help wholesale and private-label sellers too, or mainly arbitrage?

Mainly arbitrage. Online and retail arbitrage sellers buy taxable retail goods, so shipping those purchases into a no-sales-tax state removes a real cost on every order. Wholesale and private-label sellers typically buy under a resale certificate, which already exempts the purchase from sales tax, so the prep-state tax angle adds little for them. Those sellers still use tax-free-state centers, but they pick them for price, capacity, and location rather than for the sales-tax saving itself.

Are there other taxes to know about in these tax-free states?

Yes. No statewide sales tax does not mean no tax at all. Oregon imposes a Corporate Activity Tax on business receipts, and Delaware has both a franchise tax and a gross-receipts tax. New Hampshire and Montana have no general sales tax and lighter business-tax profiles, but specifics vary. These are business-level taxes, separate from the per-purchase sales tax this facet is about, so weigh them into your decision and confirm your exact obligations with a tax professional.
