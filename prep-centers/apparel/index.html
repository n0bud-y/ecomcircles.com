Apparel is the category that quietly exposes whether a prep center actually knows soft goods or just boxes hardgoods. Clothing, shoes, and accessories are not received, labeled, and shipped the same way a sealed carton of widgets is. Each garment usually needs poly-bagging with the legally required suffocation warning, folding that does not arrive creased or crushed, hang-tag and size-strip handling that survives the trip, and FNSKU placement that does not damage the item or its packaging. A center that is excellent at boxed inventory can still wrinkle, mis-bag, or mishandle a run of t-shirts. That single requirement narrows the shortlist quickly.

That is what this page filters for. Each center carries its independently pulled Google rating and at least two verified facts, so you compare on real locations, real minimums, real founding dates, and real pricing instead of a marketing blurb. Several centers here that explicitly handle apparel, including US Prep Centers and Awesome Solutions, hold strong review scores with dozens of reviews behind them. What apparel prep actually involves, and why not every center does it

Apparel prep is a different workflow from hardgoods. Most clothing has to be poly-bagged, and any bag with a five-inch or larger opening needs a printed suffocation warning to meet Amazon and Walmart inbound rules. Items have to be folded or hung so they arrive presentable, not crushed at the bottom of a box, and some sellers want light steaming or de-wrinkling on higher-priced pieces. Hang-tags, size strips, and apparel-specific barcodes all have to be placed without damaging the garment or the bag. None of that is hard, but it is fiddly, per-unit, labor-heavy work, which is why only 53 of the 287 centers in this directory call out apparel as something they handle. A facility that batches boxed wholesale all day may simply not want soft goods, so apparel is a real selection filter, not a feature every center advertises.

What to check before you ship clothing to a prep center

Start with fit for your stage. Some apparel centers are built for volume sellers: Stock and Ship requires at least six months of Amazon selling history and 500-plus units a month, and it publishes wholesale FBA rates as low as $0.95 per unit with a 50-unit minimum per SKU, so it explicitly excludes beginners. Others are friendlier to mixed, smaller batches: MT Prairie Prep N Ship focuses on smaller units across cosmetics, home goods, and apparel and claims over six years of experience. Next, weigh pricing model, since most apparel work is per-service a la carte where poly-bagging and labeling are itemized rather than one flat rate. Then look at credentials and proximity. Frequently asked questions

How is apparel prep different from preparing regular hardgoods?

Apparel is soft, foldable, and usually has to be poly-bagged, where most hardgoods ship in their own boxes. A clothing prep center poly-bags each item, applies the required suffocation warning on any bag with a five-inch or larger opening, folds or hangs pieces so they arrive presentable, and handles hang-tags, size strips, and barcodes without damaging the garment. Some also offer light steaming. It is per-unit, labor-heavy work, which is why only 53 of the 287 centers in this directory specialize in it.

Why does clothing need a suffocation warning, and will a prep center handle that?

Amazon and Walmart require a printed suffocation warning on any poly-bag with an opening of five inches or larger, because an unmarked bag is a safety violation that can get a listing flagged or a shipment refused. The apparel centers in this list handle that as part of poly-bagging, so the warning is printed or applied correctly on every bagged item. If your clothing is not already bagged, confirm a center is bagging and labeling to current marketplace spec rather than assuming it is, since requirements change.

Are there minimums for sending apparel to a prep center?

It depends on the center. Some are built for volume: Stock and Ship, which holds a perfect Google rating across 38 reviews in this list, requires at least six months of Amazon selling history and 500-plus units a month, with wholesale rates as low as $0.95 per unit and a 50-unit minimum per SKU. Others, like MT Prairie Prep N Ship, focus on smaller units and mixed batches across apparel and other categories. Filter by your monthly volume first, then confirm any per-SKU minimum before you ship.

Can one apparel prep center handle both Amazon and TikTok Shop?

Yes, and it is worth looking for, because clothing is one of TikTok Shop's strongest categories. Awesome Solutions in New Jersey is both an official Amazon SPN partner and an official TikTok Shops partner, so it can prep the same apparel SKUs for both channels rather than splitting your inventory across two providers. When you sell clothing on more than one marketplace, a center with multi-channel partner status reduces handoffs and keeps your prep consistent across each platform's inbound requirements.

Does prepping apparel in a tax-free state help arbitrage sellers?

It can. Apparel sourced through online or retail arbitrage often passes through a prep center, and routing it through a sales-tax-free state can matter for some sellers. In this list, Stock and Ship operates in Oregon and MT Prairie Prep N Ship in Montana, both states with no sales tax. The trade-off is transit time and fit: confirm the center actually handles your apparel volume and minimums first, then weigh the tax-free location as a secondary factor rather than the deciding one.
