What is an FBA Prep Center?

An FBA Prep Center is a third-party warehouse facility that handles all product preparation required to ship inventory to Amazon fulfillment centers. This includes receiving goods from your supplier or home office, inspecting for damage and defects, applying FNSKU barcodes, poly bagging, carton labeling, and coordinating shipment directly to Amazon's inbound centers. The prep center acts as an intermediary between you and Amazon, ensuring full compliance with Amazon's evolving prep requirements.

FBA Prep Requirements Checklist (2026 Compliance)

Labeling Requirements

FNSKU (Fulfillment Network SKU) barcodes are non-negotiable. Amazon scans these at intake; missing or mislabeled barcodes trigger immediate defect fees.

  • Barcode size: Minimum 1" x 2.625" (2" wide is optimal)
  • Placement: Smooth surface in "quiet zone" (no other text/graphics within 1/8" of barcode)
  • Scannability: Barcode must scan cleanly on first attempt (no damage, wrinkles, or smudges)
  • Manufacturer barcode: If product has manufacturer UPC, cover it completely with FNSKU label
  • One barcode per unit: Multi-packs must have one FNSKU per individual item AND one on the outer packaging

Packaging Requirements

Improper packaging is a leading cause of inbound defect fees. Amazon has strict standards.

  • Box specification: Rigid, six-sided boxes only (no soft-sided packaging). Maximum 23 kg (50 lbs) per carton.
  • Poly bagging: All commingled items MUST be individually poly bagged (required as of March 31, 2026). No bulk bagging.
  • Forbidden materials: Styrofoam peanuts, plastic fill, paper packing, and bubble wrap are prohibited. Use crinkle paper or air pillows only.
  • Expiration dates: MM-YYYY format (e.g., 12-2026), 36-point font minimum. For food/supplements/beauty.
  • Weight/dimension limits: Oversize (3'+ or 50+ lbs) requires special handling and higher fees.

Product Inspection

Thorough inspection prevents defect fees and returns. Professional prep centers inspect every unit.

  • Damage assessment: Check for dents, scratches, broken seals, moisture damage. Document any issues.
  • Shelf-life verification: All dated items must have 90+ days remaining shelf-life at receipt. Reject near-expiration goods.
  • Unit count validation: Count every unit in each carton. Mismatches trigger defect fees.
  • Condition verification: Confirm item condition matches Amazon listing (new vs. used). Used items require clear disclosure.

Compliance Penalties to Avoid

These fees kick in fast. Understanding them motivates investment in professional prep.

Inbound Defect Fee

$0.32-$5.72/unit (2026 rates). Triggered by labeling failures, packaging non-compliance, damage, miscount, or expiration issues. One mislabeled carton of 100 units = $572 in fees.

Abandoned Inventory Fee

$0.60/unit average. Applied if shipment sits in inbound queue over 30 days without completion (shipment plan not finalized, items not received/processed).

Return-to-Sender Costs

If Amazon can't process your shipment (compliance issues, oversize misclassification), you pay for the return shipment ($200-$500+ depending on weight).

Stranded Inventory Consequences

If items aren't sell-through and remain in inventory, Amazon may delist them or apply long-term storage fees ($0.60-$1.20/unit annually after 6 months).

Common FBA Prep Mistakes to Avoid

Incomplete FNSKU labeling

Missing barcodes or barcodes placed in non-compliant locations (edges, corners, overlapping text). Results in immediate scanning failure and defect fees.

Incorrect carton labeling

Unit count mismatch, wrong SKU on label, or missing barcode on outer carton. Amazon rejects the entire shipment.

Oversized item misclassification

Shipping a 55-lb item without marking it as oversize. Amazon charges oversize fees retroactively ($1.50-$2.50/unit premiums).

Expired or near-expiration inventory

Sending items with <90 days shelf-life. Amazon rejects them, forces return-to-sender at your cost.

Abandoned shipments (30+ days)

Not finalizing shipment plan or leaving items in inbound queue too long. Amazon charges abandoned inventory fees ($0.60/unit).

Missing shipment plan completions

Not confirming that shipment is physically received at Amazon. Creates delays, triggers system holds, can lead to stranded inventory.

Mishandled returns documentation

Not photographing returns, not grading condition, not uploading to Amazon. Prevents reinventory and causes margin loss.

Prevention strategy: Professional prep centers have checklists, quality control, and Amazon expertise to catch and prevent all seven of these. Ecom Circles' 99%+ compliance rate reflects systematic prevention of these exact issues.
